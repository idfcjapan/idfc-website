IDFC NEXT ~Participants’ Voices~

Sugiura Aki

Sugiura Aki

—“IDFC Built Through Human Connections”

Graduate of the Department of British and American Studies, Faculty of Foreign Studies, Nanzan University. IDFC2014 executive committee member.

A conversation with IDFC2017 organizing committee member 塚本沙世 (Sayo)

Aki’s profile: IDFC2014 organizing committee vice-chair and planning

Sayo Profile: IDFC2014 Participant / IDFC2016 Vice-President and Planning / IDFC2017 Planning

Question: “What led you to become an IDFC organizing committee member?”

 

———Creating an Unprecedented International Development Field Camp for Myanmar and Japan Youth Leaders———

 

Aki: I did not originally know Hashimoto, the founder. I became involved when a friend studying abroad in the United Kingdom, who was already helping organize IDFC, invited me. We had studied development studies together at a British university, and this friend knew I was interested in international cooperation and Southeast Asia. Incidentally, I later heard that they had been recruiting mainly experienced people for the organizing team, but because I did not have that experience, I was selected for my potential. (Laughs.)

The first time I spoke with Hashimoto was later, on Skype. When we first started organizing, everyone was a stranger to me. My university was in Nagoya, while the other committee members were in Kyoto or Tokyo, so things moved forward without opportunities to meet face to face. But it was difficult to convey how I felt that way, so I went to a meeting in Kyoto. I still remember that, just that time, no one else could attend, leaving only me and Hashimoto, whom I was meeting for the first time. But because we had that time together, I properly understood his feelings about IDFC and could approach the organizing work with that understanding afterward.

 

Question: “Is there any connection between what led you to become an IDFC organizer and the development studies you studied while in the United Kingdom?”

 

Aki: “I began studying development studies after entering university, with particular opportunities to learn through studying abroad and seminars. My interest began in high school, when my English teacher told us about the civil wars in Cambodia and Rwanda and about the United Nations. The university in London where I later chose to study abroad was also well known in international development studies. Through my studies there, I began wanting to work in the field myself. The region I initially wanted to see firsthand was Southeast Asia. With those thoughts in mind, I returned to Japan, and around May, after finishing my job search, I was asked whether I would like to help run IDFC. I decided without hesitation to become an organizer.”

Question: 「 What was the most difficult or memorable thing about organizing IDFC? ？」

 

———A Period of Preparation Through Trial and Error———

 

Aki: “We had a very hard time deciding on IDFC’s theme. First, during our organizers’ retreat at Ritsumeikan University in Japan, we discussed it for more than four hours. As a result, we decided to focus on education that year, with the theme ‘2020: What Universities Should Be and What an Ideal Campus Should Look Like.’”

 

I thought I had shared the theme and exchanged views with the Myanmar organizing team beforehand. But when I actually traveled to Myanmar, they told me education would not work. I remember feeling so discouraged that I called Hashimoto in tears. Haha. Their reasons were that dealing with education was politically risky and difficult, and that they did not think an equal discussion was possible when people in Myanmar could not feel proud of their country’s education. After that, the conversation shifted, and someone suggested that business would be a good theme for the year and that they wanted to hold a business competition. Thinking, “That would not be IDFC!” I no longer knew what to do.

 

Later, Hashimoto traveled to Myanmar, and we found a middle ground between our views and settled on “Career Development.” We did not simply adopt one person’s opinion, and the theme was not one that either side was completely satisfied with. I think it was a compromise in which both sides gave something up.

 

There were many other occasions when reaching consensus was difficult. Everyone was truly distinctive, whether they were Japanese or from Myanmar, and basically, they all had strong personalities (laughs). Even so, because we shared the goal of making IDFC a success, we were able to keep going together until the end. The experiences and connections I gained through IDFC are treasures to me today.”

Question: 「 Are there any scenes from the IDFC program that stand out in your memory? 」

 

Aki: “On the first day of the program, seeing the participants engaged in serious discussions and watching the scene I had imagined come to life brought tears to my eyes. It was the moment my aspirations took shape.”

Another thing that stayed with me was that as many as three Myanmar organizers quit just before IDFC began, and even during the program it was difficult to stay in step with the Myanmar organizing team. I think this was because we had not properly shared a vision of what IDFC was. With no precedent to follow, we had not shared a clear picture of “What is IDFC?” I imagine they lost sight of that and quit. I came to realize that sharing a vision of what we are working toward is very important, but very difficult.”

Question: “Please tell us how you feel your experience at IDFC is helping you today.”

 

———The Present and the Future———

 

Aki: “I currently work in sales for systems development. I meet and work with all sorts of people, including engineers at my company and clients. To be honest, there are people I do not get along with and times when I struggle to approach my work positively. In those situations, remembering how we worked together at IDFC to adjust our course and make the camp a success, despite disagreements and mistakes really gives me strength.

 

The connections formed through IDFC somehow remain strong even after we enter working life. I find that both mysterious and part of IDFC’s appeal. These personal connections, unlike those in other organizations, are wonderful, and I hope they will remain that way.”

 

 

 

Sayo’s reflections after the interview

“Hearing about how IDFC was founded made me realize that connections between people were what made its first year a success. I heard that not only Aki but also なつき, who handled public relations for IDFC2014, joined the organizing team through an introduction from someone. Yu Hashimoto’s ability to connect people is immeasurable!!”
