1. What is IDFC?
       International Development Field Camp for Myanmar and Japan Youth Leaders
(IDFC) is a voluntary, non-incorporated organization serving as the organizing body of
the annual International Development Field Camp for Myanmar and Japan Youth Leaders. Established in 2014, marking the 60th
anniversary of diplomatic relations between Japan and Myanmar, IDFC provides a
youth-led platform that connects top student leaders from both nations to tackle shared
social challenges, build mutual trust, and develop regional solutions.




ORGANIZATION AT A GLANCE
 Full Name : International Development Field Camp (IDFC)
 Org Type : Voluntary, Non-Incorporated Student Conference Body
 Established : 2014 (60th Anniversary of Japan-Myanmar Relations)
 Core Body : Executive Committee Members (Japan & Myanmar)

Vision, Mission & Core Values
Our Vision
                                                                              IDFC
                                                                             MYANMAR - JAPAN



"Create an opportunity for exchanging ideas between the youths of Myanmar and Japan,
constructing sustainable relationships that continues for mutual future profit."



Our 5 Core Missions

     Cultural Sharing: Facilitate direct, multi-faceted understanding of each nation's
     traditional and modern youth cultures to broaden individual mindsets.
     Mutual Learning: Teach methodology for social problem-solving, exchange
     knowledge, and empower next-generation youth leaders.
     Shared Social Concern: Enable delegates to identify common social interests,
     problem awareness, and mutual goals beyond borders.
     Collaborative Action: Engage delegates in co-creating innovative solutions to real-
     world challenges through field research, workshops, and public symposia.
     Sustainable Network Building: Cultivate long-term human networks across
     students, alumni, universities, NPOs, and corporate partners.


 Core Values
     Youth-Led Leadership: Driving grassroots initiative and autonomy.

     Cross-Cultural Respect: Valuing diverse perspectives through open dialogue.

     Field-Oriented Action: Grounding solutions in direct local observation and interaction.

     Long-Term Partnership: Building enduring bilateral trust.

IDFC Founder & Purpose                                          IDFC
                                                               MYANMAR - JAPAN


While economic and political ties between Japan and Myanmar grew rapidly
following the 2011 democratic transition, opportunities for direct exchange
between young leaders remained severely limited.




IDFC was founded in 2014 by



Yu Hashimoto
Realizing that this gap could undermine
future bilateral partnership, Hashimoto
launched IDFC to establish a sustainable
youth platform where future leaders build
grassroots trust and problem-solving skills
early in their careers.

2014

       student conference bridging Japan and
       Myanmar, IDFC held its inaugural
       session in Yangon.


       In 2016, the Plenary Session convened
2016   in Mandalay under the theme
       "Community Development."




2017
       The 2017 plenary session took place in
       Yangon, guided by the theme "Beyond
       the Change."



2018
       In 2018, the main conference was held
       in Yangon, producing its first tangible
       output: a free paper publication.

       held in both Japan and Myanmar.

IDFC 2027 At A Glance
IDFC
MYANMAR - JAPAN

IDFC 2027 brings together bright young minds from Myanmar and Japan to learn,
collaborate and co-create solutions for education challenges across borders and gaps.

01 THEME
“Empowering Migrant Youths Through Resilience:
Discovering Essential Skills In The Dynamic World”

TARGET
University-aged Myanmar migrants in Japan and Thailand who face barriers to accessing higher education.

BRIEF PURPOSE AND INTENSION
To explore challenges, co-design practical education pathways, and build cross-border collaboration that empowers Myanmar learners to access and succeed in higher education.

02 MEMBERS & PARTICIPANTS
TOTAL PARTICIPANTS
36 participants in total

STUDENTS
18 students from each country

INCLUDING STAFFS
Including organizing committee members and support staffs from both countries.
