Vision
Young people from Japan and Myanmar learn from one another
through international collaboration
and become a bridge between the two countries.

Mission
By providing a place for active learning
and deeper mutual understanding,
we increase interest in Myanmar
and nurture people who face social challenges and take action.

Values
1. Collaboration as equals and mutual understanding
2. A commitment to continued learning and acceptance of diversity
3. Connections that endure

Background of the launch
How IDFC began

2013
After democratization in Myanmar, a previously closed country, local students expressed a need: “We want more opportunities to interact with students from other countries.”

For Japanese students too, the opportunity for international exchange in a changing Myanmar held great appeal.

IDFC founder: Yu Hashimoto
2014
IDFC established
→Its purpose was international exchange between students from the two countries

At the launch of IDFC2014

IDFC’s Journey
2014  IDFC was founded. IDFC was held for the first time in Yangon as the first student conference between Japan and Myanmar.

2016  The main conference was held in Mandalay.

2017  The main conference was held in Yangon.

2018  The main conference was held in Yangon, and a free magazine was published as the first tangible outcome.

2019  The conference was held in both Japan and Myanmar for the first time.

2020  The main conference was held in three cities: Kyoto, Yangon, and Mandalay.

2021  Affected by the COVID-19 pandemic and the coup. Online events were held.

2022  Three online events were held.

2023  In-person main conferences were held in Osaka and Tokyo.

2024  A series of in-person cultural exchange activities was held, and the main conference discussed the issue of internally displaced people.

2025  Myanmar students living in Japan were invited to the main conference in Tokyo.

2026  Regular public lectures began. The main conference was held in Bangkok, marking the first overseas main conference in six years.

Activities
Examples of activities (from last year)

Study sessions and lectures
We invited professors and people involved with Myanmar and held five online study sessions and lectures in total.

School outreach program
We held workshops at public elementary schools to help children learn about Myanmar. Members worked together to devise various ways of teaching that would interest the children.

Social gatherings
We hold occasional meals and retreats with members and alumni. We have deepened our friendships with people from other universities and older alumni who are now working professionals.
