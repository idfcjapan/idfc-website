IDFC Through the Years

2014　IDFC was founded. The first IDFC was held in Yangon as the first student conference between Japan and Myanmar.

2016　Held the main conference in Mandalay on the theme of “Community Development.”

2017　Held the main conference in Yangon under the theme “Beyond the Change.”

2018　Held the main conference in Yangon and published a free paper as the first project output.

2019　Under the theme “Seeking New Values,” held the conference in both Japan and Myanmar for the first time.

2020　Held the main conference in three cities: Kyoto, Yangon, and Mandalay.

2021　Affected by the COVID-19 pandemic and the coup. As holding the conference overseas was difficult, four online events were held.

2022　Held three online events under the theme “New Departure.”

2023　Held the main conference in person in Osaka and Tokyo, rebuilding a forum for discussion.

2024　Held a series of in-person cultural exchange events and discussed internally displaced persons at the main conference.

2025　Invited Myanmar students living in Japan and held the International Development Field Camp for Myanmar and Japan Youth Leaders in Tokyo.

2026　Began holding public lectures regularly. Held the main conference in Bangkok, the first overseas main conference in six years.

Message from the Representative
Opening up the future of Japan and Myanmar through student exchange

Keio University, third year / IDFC Japan Representative
Kazuki
From Tokushima Prefecture. A training visit to Myanmar during high school sparked his interest in Myanmar and developing countries. After entering university in April 2022, he focused his studies on economics and area studies. In the 2024 academic year, he took a leave of absence from university to undertake an internship in Cambodia. He joined IDFC2026 and, as deputy representative, helped organize the first overseas conference in six years. He became the representative of IDFC2027 in June 2026.

Founded in 2014, IDFC enters its 13th year this year. IDFC was established on the occasion of Myanmar’s transition to civilian rule and the 60th anniversary of diplomatic relations between Japan and Myanmar. However, the circumstances surrounding exchanges between Japan and Myanmar have changed completely since then. Travel restrictions caused by the spread of COVID-19 and changes in the situation in Myanmar led to a period when the exchanges we had built up were interrupted.

Even under those circumstances, IDFC continued to explore ways to sustain student exchanges linking Japan and Myanmar. Starting with online events and expanding participation to Myanmar university students and high school students living in Japan, we have gradually restored the reach of our activities.
Last year, the executive committee worked together on preparations to fulfill our long-held hope of holding an overseas main conference for the first time in six years. We also received tremendous support and guidance from former members. These accumulated efforts led to the conference in Bangkok, Thailand.

This year is an important milestone, as we also work to establish a local organization. Building on the exchanges we have developed, we hope to further expand opportunities for new connections and learning. In IDFC2027, we will work to establish the Myanmar-side organization in Southeast Asia while continuing to hold lectures, interact with Myanmar people living in Japan, and share information in Japan. Through these efforts, we aim to realize IDFC’s vision: “Young people in Japan and Myanmar learn from one another through international collaboration and become a bridge connecting the two countries.” I myself feel the weight of that responsibility keenly once again.

I believe IDFC’s significance lies in how students from Japan and Myanmar have deepened mutual understanding by focusing on society, culture, and people’s daily lives, rather than viewing Myanmar solely through issues such as aid and refugees. Because our organization began during the transition to civilian rule, when Myanmar was also called Asia’s last frontier and there were hopes for a bright future, I believe there is meaning in returning to those origins. Through candid dialogue and exchanges made possible by our position as students, we hope to nurture new relationships and connect those accumulated efforts to the future development of Japan-Myanmar relations.

Workshops at schools

IDFC also holds workshops at schools to introduce students to Myanmar. We place particular emphasis on holding them in regional areas, working with children and students who often have limited opportunities to encounter not only Myanmar but also settings such as other countries and universities. We believe that holding workshops in these places can both introduce Myanmar and help us consider the information gap between urban and rural areas in Japan.

We adjust the workshop content each time to suit the students’ grade level. By designing lessons around material that interests students, we create opportunities for them to feel a little closer to Myanmar.

A year of activities with IDFC

IDFC’s activities extend beyond holding the main conference. Throughout the year, we carry out many kinds of activities, including lectures and study sessions, workshops at schools, exchanges with Myanmar residents in Japan, and sharing information. Through these efforts, we value deepening our own understanding of Myanmar while also expanding opportunities for learning and interest beyond our organization.

IDFC regularly holds public lectures with invited speakers and internal study sessions to broaden interest in and understanding of Myanmar in Japan. IDFC2026 held five lectures and study sessions in total, covering Myanmar’s political history, the Myanmar community in Japan, Japanese-language education, and the realities of educational support.

Repeated opportunities to learn from diverse perspectives help participants deepen their knowledge and also feel a closer connection to Myanmar.

Sharing information

IDFC Main Conference
12 conferences held to date
6 host cities to date
At least 441 participants to date

* Conference and participant counts include online events.

The main conference is IDFC’s central activity. It brings together students from Japan and Myanmar to deepen their understanding of each other’s societies, cultures, and challenges through lectures, discussions, and exchanges. Its great significance lies in being a place where students can openly exchange views and build new perspectives and relationships, as well as gain knowledge. Through this conference, IDFC aims to help young people from Japan and Myanmar develop connections as they learn.

Host cities to date:
Yangon
Mandalay
Tokyo
Kyoto
Osaka
Bangkok

The main conference also goes beyond making recommendations. IDFC continues its activities throughout the year, examining recommendations that emerge from the conference and working to put them into practice. In the past, one recommendation led to the publication of a free magazine. Turning discussions into concrete outcomes instead of letting them end with the event is a distinctive feature of IDFC.

IDFC2026 President
Honoka

The decision to hold the IDFC2026 main conference in Thailand began with our wish to rebuild opportunities for exchange between students—the original purpose of IDFC—at a time when we cannot travel to Myanmar for safety reasons.

The “main conference” gives students from Japan and Myanmar an opportunity to consider the relationship between their countries on equal terms. Beyond simply becoming friends, I believe that crossing cultural boundaries, living together, and exchanging ideas can bring lifelong bonds and new perspectives.

At IDFC, we make the main conference happen by repeatedly asking questions such as: “Will this benefit students from both Japan and Myanmar?” “Is this really the form of exchange people want?” and “Is this theme appropriate?” We plan it ourselves and bring it to life. In that process, you too should be able to have an experience that changes your life.

Last year’s main conference

“It was a precious opportunity to experience the essence of ‘dialogue.’ I am extremely proud to have been involved in this conference!”

Discussion
Students from Japan and Myanmar discuss the theme together. The discussions are in English, but everyone is kind and provides plenty of support, so it is okay even if you are worried about your language skills!

Meals and parties
As we talk over meals together, we naturally become friends! People often make lifelong friends ♡

“I think it was very meaningful that we were able not only to introduce our cultures but also to discuss higher education for migrants in each other’s countries. It was particularly good to deepen our knowledge together through fieldwork and lectures.”

“It is a well-networking community to build further actions and the activities arrangement is so good, they so vary and give powerful insights.”

Cultural introductions
Introducing each other’s cultures is one of the most popular programs at the main conference ✨. You might even get to wear Myanmar clothing or play an instrument! Think about which aspects of Japanese culture you would like to introduce 🇯🇵

Guests and alumni
At the main conference, we invite a variety of guests for programs such as lectures and shared meals. You can also connect with people further along in many different careers and impressive people you would not ordinarily have the chance to meet!
