IDFC2025 main conference held

IDFC2025 International Development Field Camp for Myanmar and Japan Youth Leaders: Main Conference Report

Purpose and Aims of the Conference

Since the military coup in 2021, exchanges between students in Japan and Myanmar have faced difficult circumstances. About four years have passed since the coup, and students in Myanmar continue to raise their voices even as their freedom is suppressed under military rule. For the past several years, the main conference had been held with Japanese students only. This year, however, we invited Myanmar students living in Japan and deepened discussion and exchange in Tokyo through a format combining in-person and online participation.

Whenever society undergoes major change, the voices of students and young people are always present. I hope the discussions at the main conference and the connections among students resonate with the voices of students in Myanmar, Japan, and around the world, and that democracy and peace come to Myanmar.

  Keynote speeches

Speaker

Professor 根本敬, Professor Emeritus at Sophia University

“Myanmar's Present and Future: Considering Its Historical Background”

 

 

Topics and discussion format for the main conference

At the main conference, students from Japan and Myanmar formed seven mixed groups of two to six people each to discuss the following topics.

List of discussion topics

Topic ① Educational inequality in Myanmar

  • Educational disparities between ethnic groups

  • Comparing the education systems of Japan and Myanmar

  • Ensuring equal access to education

Topic ② Living together with Myanmar people in Japan

  • Living together with Myanmar residents in Japan

  • Issues with the Foreign Technical Intern Training Program

  • Discussion on refugee recognition

Topic ③ Agriculture in Myanmar and Japan

  • Sharing and applying agricultural technology

  • Economy and Productivity Improvement

  • Climate Change and Sustainability

IDFC2025 Volunteer Activity Report

Activity overview

In December 2025, as members of the Japanese Executive Committee for IDFC2025, we volunteered at the “Winter Clothing Giveaway for Myanmar People” held in Nagata Ward, Kobe, and organized by the nonprofit organization Myanmar KOBE.

Purpose of Activities

This activity aims to support Myanmar people living in Kobe by distributing winter clothes and futons to help with daily life, while also promoting exchange between Japanese and Myanmar people. Particular emphasis was placed on supporting the growing number of Myanmar people living in Japan and deepening understanding of multicultural coexistence.

Activities

  • Dates : December 8, 15, and 22, 2025 (Sundays)

  • Venue : Futaba Gakusha (Nagata Ward, Kobe)

  • Activities ：

    • Transporting, sorting, and distributing winter clothes and bedding

    • Interaction with and support for visitors

    • Venue setup and operational support

Scenes from our activities

Many people from Myanmar came that day to receive clothing and bedding for the approaching cold season. We helped sort the clothing and hand suitable items to visitors. We also had the opportunity to talk with the recipients and hear about their living situations and experiences in Japan.

In particular, our exchanges with younger people from Myanmar gave us valuable time to deepen mutual understanding by discussing life and work in Japan and our dreams for the future.

Learning through participation

  • We came to appreciate the growing number of Myanmar people in Japan and the need to support them.

  • Learn the importance of interacting across cultural differences.

  • Through volunteering, we recognized the importance of expanding the circle of support.

Future outlook

As IDFC, we believe it is important to continue our relationships with the Myanmar community and expand our support activities and opportunities for exchange. We will also consider actively participating in events that promote multicultural coexistence.

Acknowledgments

We sincerely thank everyone at the nonprofit organization Myanmar KOBE for planning and running this activity. We also thank everyone who helped with the collection of winter clothing.

Multicultural Coexistence Workshop: “My Neighbor Is a Foreigner”

—Event Report

On September 22, 2024, we took part in the workshop “My Neighbor Is a Foreigner,” organized by the nonprofit Link to Myanmar. Through visits to Myanmar households and discussions, this event addressed issues relating to multicultural coexistence.

Event schedule

  • 13:00 Meet at Oppama Station
  • 13:15 Visits to Myanmar families begin
  • 14:30 End of Visit
  • 15:00 Arrival at Kanazawa District Center / Discussion begins
  • 16:30 End of discussion

Scenes from home visits

We visited local Myanmar households and learned directly about their daily lives and culture.

Two Myanmar guests and six participants deepened their connections while eating Myanmar food together.

Participants had opportunities to ask questions during the visits and showed interest in life in Japan, cultural differences, and Myanmar customs.

 

Discussion Theme

In the discussion following the visit, participants split into three groups to discuss the following themes.

  1. They do not follow the garbage rules!
  2. The foreign residents in my neighborhood are making terrible noise!
  3. What if you end up working in the same workplace as someone from another country?

These topics were based on actual difficulties that local residents encounter while living alongside foreign residents. Participants discussed in depth how they would respond to each problem and the challenges Japan faces in living together with foreign residents. After the discussions, each team gave a presentation and received comments and feedback from the speaker.

Participant voices (planned)

Here are reflections from three IDFC members who participated in the event.

「 It was my first time visiting the home of a foreign resident in Japan, and it was a very valuable opportunity!

Through various interviews, we received candid answers about what surprised them when they came to Japan and why they came here.

“We did more than simply visit homes: afterward, we invited a speaker and held a discussion. It really made us think carefully about the problems we face now and what we should do going forward!”
– ゆりこ

 

“The Myanmar food prepared by the Myanmar people we actually visited combined many different flavors, including sourness and spiciness, and was a very distinctive experience. The cuisine of neighboring Thailand is popular in Japan, but Myanmar cuisine is not yet widespread. I hope that once the civil war ends and things settle down, Myanmar cuisine will gradually become more widespread in Japan.

In the discussion afterward, we talked about problems that arise from accepting foreign residents. There are various problems, such as waste disposal, but people from many countries come to Japan, not only Myanmar. Thinking about the future, I hope Japan can become a country where Japanese people and foreign residents can both live a little more comfortably.”

- and this one here

 

 

“I had a very enjoyable time participating in this workshop. I was particularly impressed by how attentively the lecturers listened to our opinions and helped us deepen the discussion. I feel that my thinking was refined and my perspective broadened.

The account of scheduled power outages in Myanmar was also particularly shocking. I was surprised to learn that the Myanmar government is enforcing electricity conservation because it lacks funds. Learning about these circumstances made me reconsider the differences between Japan and Myanmar and the challenges of coexistence.”

Through this event, participants experienced the importance of multicultural coexistence in everyday life and learned perspectives for addressing real challenges.

Myanmar Film Festival 2024 — Event Report

On September 21, 2024, IDFC members attended the Myanmar Film Festival at JICA Global Plaza in Ichigaya. Organized by NGO SOSIA, the festival ran from 10 a.m. to 7 p.m., with moving, thought-provoking film screenings and guest talks.

Highlights of the day

The film festival began with a screening of “山の医療団”, followed by a documentary produced by NGO SOSIA. In the afternoon, “僕の帰る場所” resonated deeply with the audience, depicting refugee issues and the hardships of daily life. Next, “白骨街道” portrayed Myanmar’s complex social and political situation.

 

At lunchtime, authentic Myanmar cuisine was served, allowing us to enjoy a cultural experience as well as the film. At the end of the event, we shared dinner with the organizers and other participants, enjoying a warm exchange of impressions and opinions about the film.

 

IDFC members who went to help with the event

Here are the impressions of two IDFC members who attended the film festival.

“It was my first time watching a film about Myanmar, and it made me reconsider the issues faced by foreign residents in Japan and the fact that there had been a war in Myanmar. I learned a great deal. Most IDFC activities involve Japanese members, so it was wonderful to hear valuable stories from a variety of people! I would definitely like to take part in another event!”
– Yuriko

“I was deeply impressed by this film, which pursued a very realistic portrayal. It was also wonderful to see everyone taking an interest in Myanmar through their activities and to have so many opportunities to hear about life there. I have not been to Myanmar yet, but it made me want to visit if I have the chance.”
– じょうたろう

Closing remarks

This film festival was a valuable opportunity to understand Myanmar’s current situation through film. Besides its educational aspects, it deepened exchanges among participants, making it a wonderful day that increased interest in Myanmar. Please keep an eye on our upcoming events too!
