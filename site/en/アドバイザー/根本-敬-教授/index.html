Advisors

Takashi Nemoto, Professor Emeritus

The exchanges you have during university give you a direction in life

根本　敬

 

Professor Emeritus, Faculty of Global Studies, Sophia University. Specialist in modern Burmese history.

Japanese interest in Myanmar (Burma) has grown in recent years. In many cases, however, that interest remains limited to profit-oriented “business” or “tourism” focused only on enjoying the moment. As university students, you can freely use the fortunate period before entering working life. You have time to think freely without being constrained by the needs of companies or government offices, to ask questions, and, through exchanges with others free of vested interests, to discover how you want to live and the important values you can share with others. You must use this meaningful time wisely and must not waste it. If you are interested in Myanmar, first learn about its culture, history, and challenges through reading, and then interact directly with its people. IDFC is a valuable “place” for interaction created together by university students from Japan and Myanmar. Through participation in IDFC projects, build “equal relationships” free of vested interests—something that is difficult to experience once you enter working life. This will surely give you direction in life and help deepen your ability to live it.
